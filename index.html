        Know your pistachios

        The green jewel of the glen

        Pistachios have been grown across the Middle East and Central Asia for thousands of years and travelled west along with saffron, sesame and citrus. Their colour, sweetness and gentle richness make them as at home in savoury dukkah and pilafs as in pastries and ice cream.

        Buy them shelled and unsalted for cooking, and taste one first: they should be crisp and sweet, never soft or bitter. Toast in a dry pan for 3–4 minutes to deepen the flavour, and store in an airtight jar in the fridge or freezer so their natural oils stay fresh.

        Kerman

        The large, golden-shelled pistachio most common in US stores.

        Sicilian

        Small, vivid green and intensely flavoured; prized for desserts.

        Persian

        Long, rich kernels often sold roasted and salted for snacking.

        The Pistachio Glen way of eating

        At the table

        How a Mediterranean meal unfolds

        Meals here are rarely rushed. They move in gentle stages, and the table fills and empties several times before anyone thinks about leaving.

        Mezze to share

        Dips, bread, olives and crunchy vegetables arrive first, so everyone can graze while the rest of the meal cooks.

        Something from the grill or pot

        Skewers, a tray of roasted vegetables or a slow-cooked stew forms the heart of the meal.

        Salads and grains

        Herby salads and pilafs sit alongside, adding freshness and texture rather than following as a separate course.

        Fruit, sweets and tea

        Seasonal fruit, a small sweet and a pot of mint tea or coffee bring the meal to a slow close.

        Olive oil, simply

        Which olive oil, and when?

        Olive oil is the backbone of this kitchen. You don’t need a cupboard full of bottles; two good ones cover almost everything.

        Extra virgin for finishing

        Fruity, peppery and full of character. Drizzle it over hummus, salads, grilled vegetables and bread, where you can really taste it.

        Everyday olive oil for cooking

        A milder, more affordable oil for sautéing onions, roasting vegetables and shallow frying at moderate heat.

        Keep it fresh

        Store away from light and heat with the cap tightly closed, and use within a few months of opening for the best flavour.

        Good questions

        Mediterranean cooking, answered

        What is mezze?

        Mezze is a spread of small dishes served together for sharing, found across the eastern Mediterranean and Middle East. Think dips such as hummus and baba ganoush, warm bread, olives, salads and a few hot bites. It can be a starter or the whole meal.

        Are pistachios the same as other nuts for allergy purposes?

        Pistachios are tree nuts. Anyone with a tree nut allergy should avoid them and check labels carefully, as they often appear in spice blends, desserts and pastries. Our recipes always list pistachios clearly.

        What is sumac and how do I use it?

        Sumac is a deep red spice made from dried berries. It tastes tangy and lemony, so it’s sprinkled over salads, eggs, grilled vegetables, hummus and flatbreads. It’s also a key part of za’atar.

        How far ahead can I prepare a mezze spread?

        Most dips taste even better made a day ahead and kept covered in the fridge. Cut vegetables and herbs a few hours before, and warm the bread and any hot dishes just before serving.

        Do I need special equipment for this kind of cooking?

        Not at all. A heavy frying pan, a saucepan, a baking tray and a sharp knife will cover almost every recipe. A food processor is helpful for dips, and a pestle and mortar is lovely for spices.
