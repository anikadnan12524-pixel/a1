The Mediterranean pantry

The spices, staples and grains that make Mediterranean and Middle Eastern home cooking easy, plus how to cook, store and get the best from them.

Spice shelf

Six spices worth knowing

Buy spices in small amounts from a shop with a fast turnover, keep them in airtight jars away from the stove and replace ground spices every six months or so.

Sumac

Tangy, lemony red berries. Sprinkle over salads, eggs, grilled meat and hummus.

Aleppo pepper

Mild, fruity chilli flakes with gentle heat. Lovely on eggs, dips and roasted vegetables.

Cumin

Warm and earthy. Toast the seeds before grinding for the fullest flavour.

Za’atar

A blend of wild thyme, sumac and sesame. Mix with olive oil for bread dipping.

Cinnamon

Used in savoury rice, stews and meat dishes as well as sweets.

Saffron

Precious threads for golden rice and desserts. Steep in warm water before using.

Store cupboard

The staples on our shelves

  • Olive oil: Extra virgin for finishing; regular for cooking.
  • Tahini: Sesame paste for hummus, dressings and sweets.
  • Pomegranate molasses: Sweet-sour syrup for dressings and glazes.
  • Chickpeas and lentils: Tinned and dried, for dips, soups and pilafs.
  • Bulgur and freekeh: Quick-cooking grains for salads and sides.
  • Preserved lemons: Salty, intense lemon flavour for tagines and dressings.
  • Yogurt: For dips, marinades and cooling sauces.
  • Pistachios, almonds and walnuts: For dukkah, pilafs, salads and desserts.

Herbs

Keeping fresh herbs fresh

  • Parsley, coriander and mint: trim the stems and stand them in a glass of water in the fridge, loosely covered with a bag.
  • Dill: wrap in a slightly damp paper towel and keep in a container in the fridge.
  • Basil: keep it out of the fridge, in water, like a bunch of flowers.
  • Use the stems: chop parsley and coriander stems into salads, dressings and stews.
Food safety

Safe and simple

  • Keep yogurt dips, labneh and cooked grains chilled and eat within 3–4 days.
  • Don’t leave mezze out for more than two hours, or one hour on a hot day.
  • Cook chicken to 74°C (165°F) in the thickest part.
  • Cool cooked rice quickly and refrigerate within an hour; reheat only once until piping hot.
